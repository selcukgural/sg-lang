## 198. A program at a terminal of its own, a terminal made raw, and its size

Choices 2027 and 2028 were approved with 196 ([26 November](2026-11-26.md#slice-196)): a pseudo terminal for a
started program, and the raw mode of a terminal tested through it. The details were proposed and approved on 27
November 2026:

2042. **Its ends are kept by the `Process`** (in place of the separate `PseudoTerminal` of 2027):
     `Process.takeTerminalReader()` → `StreamReader?`, `takeTerminalWriter()` → `StreamWriter?`, and
     `resizeTerminal(int rows, int columns)` → `Result<void, IoError>`. The pseudo console of Windows keeps the
     pipe of what it shows open until it is closed (`ClosePseudoConsole`); held by the `Process`, it is closed
     when the program has ended, which a separate value taken out of it could not promise.
2043. **`Command.terminal(int rows, int columns)`**: every stream not sent elsewhere goes to the terminal; one
     sent elsewhere (`outputTo`, `pipeError` and the others) stays there on Linux and macOS. On Windows, whose
     pseudo console takes all three streams or none, a stream sent elsewhere as well is `InvalidInput` where it
     starts.
2044. **A session of its own**, whose controlling terminal this is: the byte 3 written is `SIGINT` to the group in
     front at it, and job control works inside. `groupId` and `signalGroup` reach its group. With `foreground` a
     panic where it starts (as `foreground` with `newSession`, 197); with `newSession` or `newGroup` nothing more;
     in a `Pipeline` a panic.
2045. **Started with `fork` and `exec` on Linux and macOS** (the child of 2034 again): `TIOCSCTTY` makes the
     terminal that of the session, which `posix_spawn` cannot ask for on macOS; one way for both. The interpreter
     does it in `pre_exec`.
2046. **Only `start`**: `run`, `output`, `status` and their async forms of a program at a terminal of its own are
     a panic, since no one would read the terminal. What `output` would read through one (as `script` does) is in
     what is not here yet.
2047. **The end of its reader, and its bytes**: the reader ends once the program, and those it started that still
     hold the terminal, have ended; Linux says so with `EIO`, which the reader takes as its end. A `"\n"` the
     program writes comes as `"\r\n"`; what is written to the writer is shown back unless the program turned that
     off. The pseudo console of Windows writes sequences of its own among what the program wrote, so its bytes are
     not those of Linux and macOS. A terminal holds little: what the program writes is read while it runs.
2048. **`resizeTerminal`**: `TIOCSWINSZ`, and the program gets `SIGWINCH`; on Windows `ResizePseudoConsole`. Rows or
     columns outside 1 to 65535 are `InvalidInput`; after the end, nothing.
2049. **`Terminal.rawMode()`** → `Result<RawMode, IoError>`, of the input of this program (descriptor 0;
     `NotATerminal` when it is not one), as `cfmakeraw` makes it: no echo, no wait for a line, Ctrl-C and Ctrl-Z
     as the bytes 3 and 26, and `"\n"` not turned into `"\r\n"`. `RawMode : Disposable`, whose `restore(this)`
     puts the terminal back, as its end does. On Windows `SetConsoleMode`: the input without line input, echo and
     processed input, with the sequences of a terminal (`ENABLE_VIRTUAL_TERMINAL_INPUT`); the output with
     `ENABLE_VIRTUAL_TERMINAL_PROCESSING` and `DISABLE_NEWLINE_AUTO_RETURN`.
2050. **Put back by the language**: the end of the program, a panic, and a signal that would end it with no
     listener (`SIGHUP`, `SIGINT`, `SIGQUIT`, `SIGTERM`) put back the settings seen at the first `rawMode`.
2051. **`Terminal.size()`** → `Result<TerminalSize, IoError>`, new: the rows and columns of the terminal the output
     of this program goes to (`TIOCGWINSZ`; the window of the console on Windows); `NotATerminal` when it is not
     one.
2052. **Async**: the reader and the writer of a terminal are those of a pipe. No API of keys: `Stdin` reads them,
     and the sequences a key sends are the program's to read.
2053. **Tests and a measurement**: the golden programs below, a test of the command for the terminal put back, ten
     megabytes through a terminal, and the speed of a terminal against C (`posix_openpt` and a loop of `read`),
     and of a start at one, small, medium and large.

What was built. `Command` keeps the rows and the columns and gives them to `System.start` as `ways[8]` and
`ways[9]`, with way 7 (the terminal) for each stream not sent elsewhere and a session of its own on Linux and
macOS; `made` grew to eight numbers: the end read, the end written, and what names the terminal to two new calls,
`System.resizeTerminal` and `System.closeTerminal`. Three more serve `Terminal`: `System.terminalRaw`,
`System.terminalRestore` (the settings travel as sixteen numbers, which hold a `struct termios` of Linux or macOS,
or the two modes of a console) and `System.terminalSize`. Linux and macOS open the pseudo terminal with
`posix_openpt`, set its size, and start the program through the child of 2034, which makes it the terminal of the
session after `setsid`; the end the program got is closed in the parent, so the reader comes to its end with the
program. The end kept is read, written and resized through descriptors of their own; `write` waits for room
where another made the descriptor non-blocking (the reader of a terminal shares it with the writer). On Windows the
pseudo console is found in `kernel32.dll` when first asked for, so a program still starts on a Windows without
it; the program gets handles that name nothing for its three streams, or it would get those of this program
where they were sent elsewhere and write there; the pool of threads of the system closes the console as soon as
the program ends, so that the reader comes to its end without waiting for `Process.wait`, under a lock that
`resizeTerminal` takes too. The interpreter on Windows starts such a program with `CreateProcessW` itself, since
std's `Command` cannot give it a pseudo console; the command line is quoted as compiled code quotes it. A
`Process` lets go of the ends of its terminal only when it ends, not at `wait`: the pseudo console takes the end
of its keys as its own end, and would end the program.

Decided on the way, in the spirit of the choices. On Windows a console tells a program of a new size among the
events of its input, not as a signal: there the program gets no `Signal.WindowChange`, and the pseudo console
draws its whole screen again. Ctrl-D ends the input only on Linux and macOS. Closing the writer on Windows ends
the program. A program at a terminal of its own is in no group of its own on Windows (`groupId` is `null`).
`Stdin.isTerminal()` in an async function was taken for a read that holds the thread (since 182); asking whether
a stream is a terminal waits for nothing, and is allowed there now.

Found and left: in the interpreter, an async read of the pipe of another program, and so of its terminal, holds
the run while it waits, so a task that reads while another writes to the same program waits for ever; compiled
code waits on the loop of the network. The tests write first and read after.

**Measured** (release builds; median of seven runs; the program at the terminal is the system's `head -c N
/dev/zero` for the reads and `true` for the starts; C opens the terminal itself, forks, `setsid`, `TIOCSCTTY`,
`exec`, and reads in blocks of 64 KB as SG does):

macOS, M-series:

| Case | C | SG | SG / C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 MB through a terminal | 14.3 ms | 13.9 ms | 0.97 |
| 64 MB | 634.5 ms | 608.4 ms | 0.96 |
| 512 MB | 5,025.3 ms | 4,917.1 ms | 0.98 |
| `true` at a terminal of its own, 100 times | 192.6 ms | 169.0 ms | 0.88 |
| the same, 1,000 times | 1,926.5 ms | 1,718.7 ms | 0.89 |
| the same, 5,000 times | 9,897.7 ms | 8,982.0 ms | 0.91 |

Linux (aarch64 in a container on the same machine):

| Case | C | SG | SG / C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 MB through a terminal | 4.2 ms | 4.1 ms | 0.99 |
| 64 MB | 227.4 ms | 213.7 ms | 0.94 |
| 512 MB | 1,781.0 ms | 1,716.5 ms | 0.96 |
| `true` at a terminal of its own, 100 times | 22.6 ms | 25.7 ms | 1.14 |
| the same, 1,000 times | 212.8 ms | 247.5 ms | 1.16 |
| the same, 5,000 times | 1,096.1 ms | 1,259.3 ms | 1.15 |

A terminal carries what it carries: SG reads as fast as C. A start at one is 15% slower than C on Linux, about 33
µs each: SG's child is the careful one of 2034 (a pipe that tells the parent why `exec` failed, every signal held
off across the `fork`, the signals of a terminal set back), where the C of the measurement does none of that. Its
list of open descriptors, read from `/proc/self/fd` before each `fork` for when `close_range` is missing, is now
read only where `close_range` does not work (asked once), which took 2% off. Written in [the slower
places](../../benchmarks/slower-than-c.md).

**Tests**: `ok_process_terminal` (what the program sees, its output as the terminal turns it, keys shown back and
read, Ctrl-C caught by a listener, Ctrl-D the end of its input, another size seen by it, `resizeTerminal` after the
end, a reader never taken, sizes of 0 and 70000, a stream sent elsewhere); `ok_process_terminal_raw` (bytes one by
one with nothing shown back and Ctrl-C a byte, lines and echo after `restore`, a panic while raw put back as
`stty -a` shows after it, and `NotATerminal` here); `ok_process_terminal_end` (1, 1,000 and 100,000 lines of 99
bytes, every one counted, read by async calls; keys typed ahead of a program that reads two lines);
`panic_process_terminal_run`, `panic_process_terminal_foreground`, `panic_process_terminal_pipeline`,
`panic_process_terminal_resize`; and in tests/cli.rs `a_raw_terminal_put_back_at_the_end`, which runs
tests/terminal/raw_end.sg at a terminal made by `script`, interpreted and compiled, ended by a panic, by `exit`
and by `SIGTERM` while raw, with `stty -a` after each. What the golden programs print does not differ between the
systems (the sequences of the pseudo console are taken out, and where the systems differ the program says whether
each did what it does): their `.out` was checked on Windows, interpreted and compiled. `sg-test-tool` has the
steps `tty`, `winch`, `raw=N`, `rawpanic` and `many=N`.
