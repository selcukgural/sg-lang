## 153. Time, random numbers and the log measured against Rust and Zig

Choices made in the hundred-and-fifty-third slice (time, random numbers and the log measured against Rust and
Zig). 1667 to 1671 were approved at its start, 1672 while the trials were written, 1673 to 1677 after the
profile of the first measurement, between 21 and 22 November 2026:

1667. **The rivals.** For `std/time`, Rust's `chrono` 0.4.45; Zig's library reads the clocks but has no
     calendar before 1970, no ISO 8601 and no time zones, so its program writes the calendar and the text
     by hand, with `std.fmt`, and asks the C library's `localtime_r` for the time zone, and the table says
     so. For `std/random`, Rust's `rand` 0.10 with `rand_xoshiro` 0.8's `Xoshiro256StarStar` from
     `seed_from_u64`, which is SG's generator, xoshiro256\*\* seeded with SplitMix64, and gives the same
     numbers; Zig's `std.Random.DefaultPrng` is xoshiro256++, other numbers, so nothing the programs print
     depends on the numbers drawn. For `std/log`, Rust's `log` 0.4 with `env_logger` 0.11, given the form of
     SG's lines; Zig's `std.log` with a `logFn` that writes the same lines, its own having no time.
1668. **The cases of time**, chosen before the numbers were seen: ten million moments between 1900 and
     2200, a quarter of them whole seconds and the others with milliseconds, microseconds or nanoseconds,
     each written in ISO 8601 into a text kept from one to the next; 100 MB of moments in RFC 3339 with
     offsets and fractions of 0 to 9 digits, from a new maker, `trials/inputs/time.sg`, each read and taken
     to UTC; a million moments seen in the time zone of the system, `TZ=Europe/Istanbul`; the monotonic
     clock read ten million times.
1669. **The cases of random numbers**: a hundred million numbers of 64 bits, the bits set counted; a hundred
     million numbers up to 1,000 in a histogram; 100 MB of bytes, 64 KB at a time into one buffer; a
     million tokens of 64 hex digits from the random bytes of the system (Rust's `SysRng`, Zig's
     `Io.random`, which is a generator for keys kept in the process and seeded by the system). What is
     printed is whether the bits, each bar and the mean of the bytes lie within six standard deviations.
1670. **The cases of the log**, each line on the standard error written into a file: lines at the level
     Info; lines at Debug while the log writes from Info, none written and the text of none made; four
     tasks writing at once. `measure.py` holds every line to its form, a time to the millisecond, the level
     and the message, and the messages, in the order of their text, to be the same everywhere.
1671. **The rest as the frame has it** (1631, 1634, 1635): the tests run the trials small, a case more than
     1.10 times the faster of Rust and Zig is profiled, and the ways a program fails are not measured.
1672. **A million lines, not ten**, in the first and third cases of the log: each line is one write of the
     system in all three languages, ten million took 20 to 45 seconds a run, and a million (60 MB) show the
     same. The lines that are off stay a hundred million.
1673. **What was made faster, for every program, with the same output.**
     - A number written with a least count of digits (`{n:D2}`, `%.2lld` in C) is written digit by digit
       into the text, as one without was, not through `snprintf`: each part of a date went through the
       parsing of a format. The digits are counted first and each written once into the room of the text,
       the zeros before them by the same loop, with no `memcpy` or `memset` of a few bytes.
     - On macOS the lines of `printError` from several threads wait for each other on an `os_unfair_lock`
       of the program's own, not on the lock of C's stream, which waits in the kernel; the line is one
       `fwrite` as before.
     - `Random.fill` copies the eight bytes of each draw at once (`copyFrom`), and `SecureRandom.hexString`
       writes 64 digits at a time through `Hex.encodeInto`; the same bytes.

     A copy of a few bytes into a text with loads and stores of a fixed size, in place of `memcpy`, was tried
     for the reading of RFC 3339, gained nothing, and was taken out.
1674. **The monotonic clock on macOS is `CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW`**, read with `clock_gettime_nsec_np`, in compiled
     code and in the interpreter: it stops while the machine sleeps, as Linux's `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` and Rust's
     `Instant` do, where `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` of macOS went on. Ten million readings take 0.105 s in C where they
     took 0.184; the timers of tasks keep their clock.
1675. **A moment in the time zone stays with `localtime_r`**, written under "Slower than C": `chrono` reads the
     TZif file of the zone once and finds each offset itself, 3.9 times as fast; a reader of TZif of SG's own
     is a direction, Windows keeping the rules of its system.
1676. **The random bytes of the system stay the system's** (`arc4random_buf`, `getrandom`): Zig's generator
     kept in the process is 4.7 times as fast for a token, but a generator in the process has to know when the
     process is forked and keep its key out of a dump of memory; Rust's `SysRng`, which asks the system each
     time as SG does, takes four times SG's time.
1677. **Written as they are**: Zig removes a line that is off when the program is compiled, where SG reads the
     level as the program runs (and is faster than Rust); Zig's xoshiro256++ is cheaper than xoshiro256\*\*, and
     Rust's xoshiro256\*\* takes SG's time to within 1.10; reading RFC 3339 takes 1.10 times Zig's hand-written
     reader.

Before 1673 and 1674: 1.183 s to write ten million moments, 0.178 s for the clock, 0.031 s for 100 MB of
random bytes, 0.341 s for a million tokens and 4.209 s for the lines of four tasks; after, 0.490 s (Rust
0.497), 0.097 s (Rust 0.166, Zig 0.158), 0.014 s (as both), 0.242 s and 2.761 s (Rust 3.642, Zig 2.567).

Tested by `the_trial_of_time`, `the_trial_of_random` and `the_trial_of_log` (new): the SG programs,
interpreted and compiled, write 1,000 moments and read seven of RFC 3339 (before 1970, offsets of either sign,
fractions of one to nine digits) as `chrono` does, see 1,000 moments in Istanbul as `chrono` sees them where
the system has the zone, draw numbers that look random, and write lines of the log in their form, whole from
four tasks. That `Random` gives the numbers of `rand_xoshiro` was held, outside the tests, on a million numbers
and a `fill`; `ok_random_seeded` holds the first numbers of three seeds to the reference code.
The whole of the tests passes on macOS and on Linux, with 508 of the 1926 programs compiled through C and run
clean under the sanitizers, 109 of them under the thread sanitizer.
